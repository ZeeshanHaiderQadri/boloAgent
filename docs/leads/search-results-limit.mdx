---
title: "Search result limits"
description: "How Bolo collects large lead lists past Google Maps' per-query limit, and why a search can still finish below its target."
---

A lead search sends one query to Google Maps and keeps collecting until it
reaches your target or the planned queries run out.

## Why one query returns about 20 businesses

Google Maps serves roughly one page of results — about 20 businesses — for a
text search, and the scraper's deeper scrolling does not fetch more for these
queries. One query therefore cannot meet a large target on its own.

## How Bolo collects more

For a target above about 15 businesses, Bolo splits the search into **one query
per city or town** inside the location — or per neighbourhood when the location
is itself a city. The place names come from OpenStreetMap, with Wikidata
answering the same question when the OpenStreetMap query service is at
capacity. The queries run concurrently in a single scraper job and their
results are merged and de-duplicated, so "Software companies in California"
searches Fresno, Bakersfield, Riverside, and so on instead of stopping at the
first 20 hits.

A search asking for 100 leads therefore runs about seven area queries; a search
asking for 1000 runs up to 60 within its time budget. Businesses are
de-duplicated across queries and across searches: a company already in your
CRM is linked to the new search instead of being created twice.

If OpenStreetMap cannot resolve the location, the search falls back to the
single original query.

## Why a search can still finish below its target

**Completed** means the scraper finished; it does not mean the target was
reached. A shortfall is normal when:

- the category is rare in the area, so the planned cities return few matches;
- the location is fully covered and extending finds no new places;
- the search reached its **max runtime** (Search tuning on the new-search
  form) before every planned query finished. Collect the rest by extending.

Extending a search plans **additional** cities or neighbourhoods and skips the
places already searched, so it adds new businesses instead of repeating the
first query.

## How Bolo builds each query

The base query is `<category> in <location>`. If the category already ends with
the location, Bolo does not repeat it — the example above searches "Software
companies in California", not "… in California in California". Area queries
append the place name: "Software companies in Fresno, California".
