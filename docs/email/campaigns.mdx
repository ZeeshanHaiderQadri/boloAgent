An email campaign is a saved plan: a sender, a template, an audience rule and a
consent policy. Creating and previewing a campaign never sends email.

<Warning>
  Sending is off by default. `EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_SENDING_ENABLED=false` makes the
  launch endpoint answer `403`, and this release sends nothing and queues no
  recipient rows even when the flag is on. See
  [Environment Variables](/developer/environment-variables#email-campaigns).
</Warning>

## Choosing the audience

A campaign addresses one of four things:

- **Selected leads** — up to 1,000 lead ids. Ids from another organization are
  rejected; they are reported the same way as ids that do not exist.
- **A lead project** — every lead in that saved project.
- **A tag** — every lead carrying that tag.
- **A filter** — the same filters as the leads list, applied to *all* matching
  leads. A campaign cannot capture just one page; save a project for that.

## The exclusion order

Every lead the rule matches lands in **exactly one** bucket, checked in this
order:

1. `no_email` — the lead has no address.
2. `invalid_email` — the shared validator rejects it (syntax, placeholder
   domain, image extension, too long, and so on).
3. `suppressed` — the address is on the organization's suppression list.
4. `opted_out` — the lead's marketing consent is opted out.
5. `unknown` — consent is unknown. Excluded by default; admitted only with the
   include-unknown override.
6. `eligible` — consent is opted in (or unknown under the override).

A lead that fits several buckets is counted in the first one only. For example,
an opted-out lead whose address is suppressed counts as `suppressed`.

Consent is bound to the address: the resolver judges the lead's **current**
address and **current** consent, because consent is evidence about the address
the person agreed to.

## Duplicates

Once a lead is eligible, its normalized address identifies the recipient.
When several eligible leads share one address, one of them represents it —
preferring an opted-in lead over an unknown one, then the newest record. The
extra leads are reported as `duplicates_collapsed_count` and are not
recipients.

## Preview

**Preview** resolves the rule and returns every count plus a five-address
sample. Sample addresses are masked (`o***@example.com`); a preview never
returns a full address. Previews of a large filter stream leads from the
database in batches and never load them all into memory.

Previews are rate limited per organization and user. If the rate limiter's
Redis is unreachable the endpoint answers `503` rather than allowing the work.

## Consent override

The default policy addresses opted-in leads only. Including unknown-consent
leads requires a written reason. The reason is stored on the campaign with the
user, the timestamp and the consent warning shown in the UI, so the decision is
auditable later.

## Sender guidance

The campaign response shows the sender's daily cap and, once the audience is
previewed, a plain estimate such as "100 per day, this campaign will take 3
days." The cap is guidance for planning; it is not a promise about provider
throughput.

## Deleting a campaign or a sender

- A campaign can be deleted only while it is **draft** or **ready**. Anything
  further along answers `409`; cancel it instead.
- A sender cannot be deleted while a **draft**, **ready**, **launching** or
  **sending** campaign uses it (`409`). Once every campaign using it is
  completed, cancelled or failed, those campaigns are detached (the sender link
  becomes empty, the identity snapshot stays) and the sender is deleted in one
  transaction.
- A sender with message history is never hard-deleted; disable it instead so
  delivery records keep their origin.

## What launch checks

Launch is one shared check, used by the API and shown by the UI:

- a sender is chosen and verified;
- a template is chosen, its saved unsubscribe flag is true, **and** the
  sanitized HTML still contains the `{{unsubscribe_link}}` placeholder;
- `PUBLIC_BASE_URL` is set to an absolute public `http(s)` origin, not
  `localhost`;
- at least one recipient is eligible;
- if the include-unknown override admits unknown-consent leads, a written
  reason is stored.

Every failing check is reported at once, in plain language.

## Bounce ordering

A provider bounce or complaint can arrive while a message is still marked
**sending**. Apply it in order: first record the delivery result
(`sending → sent`), then apply provider feedback (`sent → bounced` or
`sent → complained`). Moving `sending` straight to `bounced` is not a valid
transition and would overwrite the send worker's result.

## Data retention

Delivery records are kept as the audit trail: each `email_messages` row retains
the rendered subject and bodies, the recipient address and the delivery
outcome. Deleting a draft campaign does not create or remove delivery history,
and finished campaigns are not purged automatically. Deleting an organization
removes its records with it.
